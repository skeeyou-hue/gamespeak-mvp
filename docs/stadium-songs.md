# Ambient stadium songs — reference list

**Status: reference only.** Nothing here is wired to anything. No playback, no
triggers, no audio files, no code. This is a shortlist to argue about now so
that if ambient sound ever gets scoped as a real feature, the music research
is already done and the hard questions have already been asked.

## Read this before anything is built on it

**Licensing is the gate, not the code.** Every song below is a commercial
recording under copyright. Playing one in a shipped app needs both a
composition licence and a master-recording licence, and the sync/mechanical
terms for a game are not the terms a stadium operates under. A ballpark plays
these under a blanket public-performance licence that does not extend to
software. Treat the whole list as *what the tradition sounds like*, not as a
playlist that can be dropped in. Realistic routes if this ever ships:
original music written to evoke the register, a production-music library, or
negotiated licences for two or three signature tracks.

**Confidence is marked, and it is not uniform.** "Documented" means the tie to
baseball is specific and well established. "Regional staple" means the song is
a fixture of the crowd culture in that country and turns up at sporting events
including baseball, but is not a baseball song as such. "Fits the register"
means it is the right music for the moment and the right tradition, and the
baseball tie is my inference, not a documented fact. Verify anything marked
"fits the register" against actual league usage — LIDOM, LVBP, LBPRC, Serie
Nacional, LMB — before treating it as tradition rather than taste.

**Nothing here is a home-run song in the way US parks mean it.** Caribbean
parks lean on live bands, dueling drums, whistles, and crowd chants far more
than on a queued track. A song-triggered model is a US broadcast convention
imported onto a culture that mostly does it with people. Worth deciding
deliberately rather than by default.

## Pre-game and general ambience

Music that plays while the park fills, between half-innings, over the
concourse. The bar is that it can loop without anyone noticing it.

| Song | Artist | Origin | Confidence | Where it fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Guantanamera | trad., popularised by Joseíto Fernández | Cuba, 1929 | Regional staple | The single most recognisable Cuban song there is. Gates opening, warm-ups. Slow enough to sit under crowd noise. |
| El Manisero (The Peanut Vendor) | Moisés Simons | Cuba, 1928 | Documented | A *pregón* — a street-vendor's cry set to music, literally about a peanut seller working a crowd. There is no better-fitting piece of music for a ballpark concourse in this tradition. |
| Chan Chan | Compay Segundo | Cuba, 1987 | Fits the register | Slow son, unhurried. Gates and batting practice, not action. |
| El Cuarto de Tula | Buena Vista Social Club | Cuba, 1997 | Fits the register | Longer, builds; good as a bed under a pre-game show. |
| Caballo Viejo | Simón Díaz | Venezuela, 1980 | Regional staple | Venezuelan standard, near-universally known. Fits LVBP ambience. |
| Compadre Pedro Juan | Luis Alberti | Dominican Republic, 1930s | Regional staple | The merengue *típico* standard. Reads as "Dominican winter ball" immediately. |
| Ojalá Que Llueva Café | Juan Luis Guerra | Dominican Republic, 1989 | Regional staple | Warmer and more modern than the *típico* material; good mid-game bed. |

## Rally — runners on, crowd standing up

Faster, brass-forward, built to be clapped along with. These are the ones that
would sit behind a bases-loaded moment.

| Song | Artist | Origin | Confidence | Where it fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| El Cumbanchero | Rafael Hernández | Puerto Rico, 1943 | Documented | Written as a fanfare and used as one for eighty years — school bands, parades, charge cues. The closest thing on this list to a purpose-built rally tune. |
| Quimbara | Celia Cruz & Johnny Pacheco | Cuba/US, 1974 | Regional staple | Relentless. Best used short — eight bars between pitches, not a full play. |
| Llorarás | Oscar D'León | Venezuela, 1975 | Fits the register | Salsa dura, huge in Venezuela. Fits an LVBP crowd on its feet. |
| Un Verano en Nueva York | El Gran Combo de Puerto Rico | Puerto Rico, 1975 | Regional staple | El Gran Combo is the house band of Puerto Rican public life. Immediately legible as PR. |
| Pégate | Ricky Martin | Puerto Rico, 2006 | Fits the register | Modern, written to be a crowd chant. Reads younger than the rest of the list. |
| A Pedir Su Mano | Juan Luis Guerra | Dominican Republic, 1990 | Fits the register | Merengue at full tilt. Dominican rally energy without being a novelty. |

## Home run, walk-off, celebration

Short, immediate, unambiguous. The requirement is that the first two seconds
already sound like a celebration — nobody hears bar nine of a home-run cue.

| Song | Artist | Origin | Confidence | Where it fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| La Vida es un Carnaval | Celia Cruz | Cuba/US, 1998 | Regional staple | The default Latin celebration song. Opens bright and stays there. Risk: it is *the* obvious pick, which cuts both ways. |
| Vivir Mi Vida | Marc Anthony | Puerto Rico/US, 2013 | Regional staple | Ubiquitous at Latin American sporting events since release. Modern rather than classic — include or not depending on how period the app wants to feel. |
| Suavemente | Elvis Crespo | Puerto Rico/DR, 1998 | Fits the register | Merengue, instantly recognisable hook. Better between innings than on the home-run trigger itself. |
| El Rey | José Alfredo Jiménez | Mexico, 1971 | Documented (Mexican context) | A defiance song, sung *at* the opposition as much as celebrated with. Strongly tied to Mexican crowds; out of place in a Caribbean-framed park. |

## Ceremonial — openers, closers, anthem-adjacent

Not action music. These carry civic weight and should be used sparingly or not
at all; misusing them reads as tone-deaf rather than atmospheric.

| Song | Artist | Origin | Confidence | Where it fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alma Llanera | Pedro Elías Gutiérrez / Rafael Bolívar Coronado | Venezuela, 1914 | Documented | Venezuela's unofficial second anthem; traditionally closes gatherings. End of game, not during. |
| Preciosa | Rafael Hernández | Puerto Rico, 1937 | Documented | Deeply patriotic. Pre-game ceremony only, and only with intent. |
| En Mi Viejo San Juan | Noel Estrada | Puerto Rico, 1942 | Documented | A song about leaving and not coming back. Closing credits, end of a season — never a rally. |

## What this list is missing

- **Actual baseball songs.** There are Spanish-language songs *about* baseball,
  and team anthems for individual winter-league clubs (Leones del Caracas,
  Águilas Cibaeñas, Tigres del Licey and the rest). I have not included any,
  because I could not vouch for specific titles and artists to the standard the
  rest of this list is held to. Someone with access to league sources should
  fill this gap — it is the most interesting category and the emptiest.
- **The chants.** *"¡Se ponchó!"*, the call-and-response, the whistling, the
  drums in the outfield. These are the real Caribbean ballpark soundtrack and
  they are not songs at all. If ambience is ever scoped for real, crowd audio
  probably matters more than any track above, and is far easier to license.
- **Non-Caribbean traditions.** The brief was Caribbean and winter league, so
  Mexican (LMB, LMP) material is represented by one entry and Japanese, Korean
  and US-Latino park traditions not at all.

## If this becomes a feature

Rough shape of the decisions, so they are not made by accident later:

- **Ambience vs. cues are different problems.** A looping bed needs one long,
  unobtrusive, cheaply licensed track. Event cues need short, punchy stings on
  specific triggers. They have different licensing, different file budgets and
  different failure modes — a bed that annoys is worse than a cue that misses.
- **Triggers, if cues happen at all.** The obvious hooks in the current code
  are a home run, a runner scoring, the third out and the end of an inning.
  Nothing in the timing layer would need to change to hang a cue on any of
  them; they are already discrete, already tested moments.
- **Muted by default.** Audio that starts on load is a bug in a browser game
  and browsers now block it anyway. Assume off, with a visible control.
